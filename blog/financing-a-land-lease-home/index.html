Financing a Land-Lease Home at Sunrise Village: What Lenders Actually Want to See

November 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 5 min read

Buyers coming from a freehold background are often surprised to learn that financing a land-lease home works a bit differently. It's not harder, exactly, but it does involve a few extra questions your lender will want answered before approving the mortgage.

The Lease Itself Is Part of the Application

Because you're financing a home that sits on leased land rather than land you own outright, most lenders will ask to see the lease agreement (or a summary of its key terms) as part of the underwriting file. They want to understand the fee, how it's reviewed, and generally how long the leasehold arrangement is expected to continue.

Not Every Lender Treats Land-Lease the Same Way

Some mainstream lenders are comfortable financing manufactured and land-lease homes with standard terms. Others apply a different down payment requirement, a shorter amortization, or simply decline this category of property. Credit unions in the Okanagan have historically been more familiar with land-lease financing than some of the national banks, since the structure is common in this region.

The practical takeaway: talk to your lender, or a mortgage broker who's specifically financed land-lease homes before, early in the process — ideally before you write an offer, not after it's already conditional. A broker who already knows which lenders are comfortable with this structure can save you a subject-removal scramble.

What to Have Ready

  • A copy of the current lease terms for the specific home, including the monthly fee.
  • The age and condition of the home itself — some lenders factor this in more heavily for manufactured homes than for a built-on-site rancher.
  • Confirmation of park approval status, since your purchase at Sunrise Village is subject to that step regardless of financing.

None of this should discourage you from buying here — plenty of buyers finance Sunrise Village homes successfully every year. It just means the paperwork looks a little different than a typical freehold purchase, and it's worth sorting out the lender question before you're deep into a conditional period.
