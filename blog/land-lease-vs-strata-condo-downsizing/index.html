Land Lease vs. Strata Condo: Which Makes More Sense When You're Downsizing?

November 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 6 min read

Downsizers evaluating Sunrise Village often have a strata condo on their shortlist too. Both can make sense — the right answer depends on what you're actually optimizing for. Here's an honest comparison.

Purchase Price & Ongoing Costs

Land-lease homes are typically more affordable to purchase than an equivalent strata condo, since you're buying the home only, not a share of the land under it. In exchange, you pay the ongoing land-lease fee instead of a strata fee. Neither fee builds equity — both are the cost of occupying the space long-term — but the trade-off shows up differently on each side of the ledger, and it's worth running real numbers for a specific home and a specific condo before assuming one is cheaper overall.

What You Actually Own

In a strata condo, you own your unit and a proportional share of the common property, governed by a strata corporation with bylaws, a council, and AGMs. At Sunrise Village, you own the home itself outright and lease the land under BC's Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act — no strata corporation, no council votes, no shared building to maintain.

Governance & Rules

Strata living means decisions get made collectively — sometimes slowly, sometimes in ways you disagree with but are bound by anyway. Sunrise Village operates under its own Rules & Regulations set by the park operator, with a more straightforward, less committee-driven structure. Some buyers strongly prefer one approach over the other.

Maintenance

A strata typically handles exterior building maintenance, roofing, and common areas through the strata fee and reserve fund. At Sunrise Village, you're responsible for your own home's maintenance, while the land-lease fee covers the broader community's shared spaces and infrastructure.

Lifestyle & Community

This is genuinely subjective, but worth naming directly: Sunrise Village's clubhouse culture — the pool, hot tub, fitness room, and billiards room — creates a different day-to-day social fabric than most strata buildings, where residents may interact far less. If community is part of what you're downsizing toward, it's worth visiting both in person before deciding.

Our honest take: there's no universally "better" option between the two — it depends on your priorities around upfront cost, governance, maintenance responsibility, and the kind of day-to-day community you want.
