What Happens to the Lease When You Sell a Home at Sunrise Village

November 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 4 min read

If you're thinking about selling your home at Sunrise Village, one question comes up almost immediately: what happens to the land lease when the home changes hands? The short version is that the lease doesn't simply transfer along with the home automatically — there's a step your buyer needs to go through first.

You're Selling the Home, Not the Lease

As the seller, you're selling the home you own. The land underneath it is leased from Sunrise Village's operator, and that lease relationship is between the park and whoever occupies the site — it doesn't pass to a new owner by default the way a deed would in a freehold sale.

Your Buyer Goes Through Park Approval Too

Just as you likely did when you bought, your buyer will need to go through the park's approval process before the sale can close — meeting with management and agreeing to the community's Rules & Regulations. This is a standard, expected step at Sunrise Village, not a sign of a complicated sale, but it does mean your closing timeline should account for it rather than assuming a purely freehold-style transaction speed.

What This Means for Timing Your Sale

  • Build in time for approval when setting your closing date with a buyer — it's an additional step beyond a typical subject-removal period.
  • Make sure your buyer's agent understands the structure early, so it isn't a surprise mid-transaction.
  • Have your current lease terms on hand to share, since buyers (and their lenders) will want to see them.

Why this is worth getting right: a sale that falls through late because a buyer wasn't prepared for park approval is avoidable. Pricing and marketing the home accurately as a land-lease property, to buyers who already understand what that means, tends to produce a smoother closing than treating it like a standard freehold listing.

We specialize in Sunrise Village specifically, which means we price, market, and manage these sales with the lease structure built into the process from day one — not treated as a late-breaking complication.
